Residential average revenue per kWh, 2020–2024

Each utility's residential sales revenue divided by its residential sales, using the values reported on EIA Form 861. This is an average across the utility's reported residential service types. It is not an individual customer's tariff or a standardized total bill.

Caveat: in states with retail choice (especially NY), customers who buy supply from a competitive ESCO have their supply charge excluded from the utility's reported revenue here — only delivery is included. This can make the blended average lower than the total price paid by a bundled or default-service customer. The chart should therefore be used as reported utility revenue per kWh, not as a standardized customer bill.

Modeled residential base-delivery bill, 2020–2024

A simple calculation using two published tariff components: the fixed monthly customer charge plus 700 kWh times the base distribution energy charge. It excludes supply, transmission, taxes, riders, and other delivery adjustments. It is not a reported total customer bill.

Sources: official utility tariffs and regulator records for Con Edison SC 1 Rate I, NYSEG SC 1, NSTAR R-1, and United Illuminating Rate R, using a December 31 snapshot. Download the source register for document names, tariff leaves, notes, and URLs.

Methodology & caveats

  • Generation data: EIA Form 923 generation by fuel, processed in PUDL's out_eia923__yearly_generation_fuel_combined table. The regional charts join EIA Form 860 plant records from core_eia860__scd_plants to identify each plant's balancing authority. See the official EIA-923 and EIA-860 source pages.
  • Residential average revenue: reported residential sales revenue and sales from EIA Form 861, processed in PUDL's core_eia861__yearly_sales table. The chart divides revenue by sales and converts the result to cents per kWh. See the official EIA-861 source page.
  • Base-delivery model: fixed charges and base distribution energy charges were transcribed from the official tariff documents listed in the source register. The displayed bill is derived as fixed charge + 700 × base energy charge.
  • Verification: on August 8, 2026, all three generated PUDL datasets matched a fresh query to displayed precision. All 20 tariff observations tied to the cited official tariff pages, and all modeled bills tied to the stated calculation.
  • Years: 2020–2024.
  • Utilities: filtered by exact EIA utility ID, not name matching, to avoid mixing in unrelated same-named subsidiaries (e.g. Con Edison's merchant generation arm).
  • These are 4 specific regulated operating utilities, not their parent holding companies. "NSTAR Electric" here is Eversource Energy's Massachusetts electric utility subsidiary only — it excludes Connecticut Light & Power, Public Service of New Hampshire, and the rest of Eversource. "Con Edison" here is Consolidated Edison Company of New York (CECONY), not the publicly-traded parent Consolidated Edison, Inc. NYSEG and United Illuminating are both Avangrid subsidiaries, so they are not independent competitors at the parent-company level — comparing all 4 charts as if they were 4 unrelated companies would be a mistake.
  • Limitation (utility-owned section): this shows generation reported directly under each utility's own EIA identity only. It does not allocate jointly-owned plants by ownership share, roll up parent/subsidiary companies, or measure purchased power, so it is not the full generation mix delivered to retail customers. PUDL's generation-fuel table uses canonicalized EIA utility identifiers reconciled across multiple EIA sources; see PUDL's data dictionary for how that canonicalization works.
  • Why the regional section exists: utility-reported generation alone does not describe the full supply mix serving retail customers. The NYISO and ISO-NE charts add regional context, but they are not utility procurement data and should not be interpreted as a customer's exact delivered fuel mix.
