Preliminary model demonstration

Does reliability help explain the ownership-price difference?

This draft follows the professors’ proposed panel approach using the data already reviewed. It compares the same utility-year observations three ways: without a reliability measure, with routine SAIDI, and with routine CAIDI.

Draft—not final and not causal. These models describe relationships in a selected sample. They do not prove that ownership or reliability causes prices.

The idea in plain language

If the ownership estimate shrinks, reliability may explain part of the gap.

Secondary question

Does reported reliability differ by ownership?

Higher SAIDI or CAIDI means worse reported reliability. These are routine measures excluding major event days and remain utility-reported outcomes.

Reading the output

Three statistical terms, simply explained

Coefficient
The estimated difference connected to one variable after the other listed variables are included.
p-value
A measure of uncertainty. This page calls an estimate “clear” only when p is below 0.05.
R²
The share of price variation tracked by the whole model. It is not one variable’s individual contribution.

Methods and limits

What was included

Primary sample

The selected 30-utility panel covers 2013–2024. The three price versions use the same complete rows with price, SAIDI, CAIDI, and a known reporting method. The displayed utility count is therefore lower than 30.

Controls

Ownership, ISO market, year, and reliability reporting method are included. DOM, NYISO, IEEE reporting, and 2013 are the reference categories.

Uncertainty

Every utility-year counts once. Standard errors are clustered by utility because repeated years for one utility are related. No customer weighting or automatic outlier deletion is used.

Why no ROI/ROE?

Audited coverage is still limited, and a cooperative has no directly comparable authorized shareholder return. ROE remains separate visual evidence rather than a panel-model variable.

What this does not prove

Ownership, reliability, market, regulation, geography, infrastructure, and customer mix can affect one another. These regressions organize the observed relationships; they do not isolate a causal ownership effect. A causal study would require a separate acquisition-based design with more ownership changes.
