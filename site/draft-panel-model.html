Ownership and bundled electricity prices

Do bundled prices differ by ownership type after accounting for state and year differences, and does adding routine SAIDI materially change the estimates?

Panel model

A panel follows the same utilities over multiple years. Each observation here is one utility in one year. The model compares ownership groups while allowing states and years to have different general price levels.

Priceit = ownershipit + statei + yeart + errorit

This is a pooled panel model, not a utility fixed-effects model. The estimates are adjusted associations, not causal effects.

Main price results

The headline models use every available price in the 42-utility regional sample. Negative estimates mean the ownership group has a lower adjusted price than DOM, the reference group.

Estimates are in inflation-adjusted 2024 cents per kWh. Brackets show 95% confidence intervals; p-values use standard errors clustered by utility.

Exploratory SAIDI check

All ownership estimates are relative to DOM. The two columns use exactly the same utility-years. The second model adds routine SAIDI, measured as outage minutes per customer excluding major-event days. Both versions also account for reliability-reporting method.

This matched sample is smaller than the main price sample because reviewed SAIDI is unavailable for some utilities and years. It is a sensitivity check, not the headline model.

Interpretation

These models test whether the observed ownership-price differences remain after broad state and year differences are considered. They do not identify why the differences exist. SAIDI is shown only to test whether adding it materially changes the ownership estimates.

Methods

  • Separate residential, commercial, and industrial models.
  • EIA bundled average revenue per kWh, adjusted to constant 2024 cents.
  • DOM is the ownership reference group; state and year indicators are included.
  • Each usable utility-year counts once; there is no customer weighting.
  • Accurate unusual values are retained; there is no automatic outlier deletion.
  • Uncertainty is clustered by utility.
