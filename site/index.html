Utility ownership, electricity prices, and reliability

Do electricity prices differ across utility ownership types? This site compares published electricity prices for 30 selected utilities in New York and New England from 2013–2024. The utilities are grouped as foreign-owned investor utilities (MTC), U.S.-owned investor utilities (DOM), and local non-shareholder utilities (COOP). The results are preliminary and descriptive: they show where prices differ and add reliability, regional fuel mix, and matched-state return-on-equity context, but do not yet establish why.

Utility histories

Each chart follows one operating utility from 2013 through 2024. Utilities stay grouped by their 2024 ownership category so their positions remain consistent across the page. Each group contains its ten largest qualifying utilities by 2024 residential customers, arranged as two rows of five.

Point colors show the ownership code in that year. United Illuminating and Narragansett Electric therefore change color when their parent ownership changes. Matched-state check: prices and year-end authorized ROE

Investor-owned utilities can earn a regulator-authorized return on the shareholder-funded part of approved rate base. Local non-shareholder utilities do not have this shareholder ROE, although they still have financing costs; cooperatives may return margins through capital credits. This section compares one DOM and one MTC utility under the same regulator in Connecticut, Massachusetts, and New York. Matching within a state makes the comparison more useful, but it remains descriptive rather than causal.

Each utility stays separate. Its full-year average price uses the left axis and year-end authorized ROE uses the right axis; both scales are held fixed across all six charts. ROE is a step line because it changes when a new decision takes effect. The two axes support a descriptive comparison of direction, not a claim that the measures are perfectly time-aligned or causal. An orange-outlined price point still marks bundled coverage below 50%.

Prices come from EIA. ROE comes from PURA, Massachusetts DPU, and New York PSC decisions, with SEC or official company filings used where they provide the clearest stable record. Each price is EIA's average for the full calendar year; each ROE is the authorized rate in force at that year's end. Hover, tap, or focus a point to open the source used for that year. Regional generation mix

These charts show the fuel used by power plants in the NYISO and ISO-NE balancing areas. They describe the regional supply environment shared by utilities—not the power owned or purchased by any one utility.

Source: plant-level net generation reported on EIA Form 923, grouped using each plant’s annual EIA balancing-authority code through PUDL. Imports are not included, so this is not the exact electricity mix delivered to customers. The 2024 totals were checked against reports published by ISO New England and NYISO.

Fuel mix can help explain changes in regional supply costs. It cannot, by itself, explain why utilities with different ownership types charge different retail prices while operating in the same regional market.

Methods and sources

  • Price: copied directly from the “Average Price (cents/kWh)” columns in Tables 6–8 of EIA’s annual Electric Sales, Revenue, and Average Price report. These tables cover bundled residential, commercial, and industrial service. The project does not recalculate these prices.
  • Coverage: bundled service for the selected customer type. In states with retail choice, this can represent only the utility’s full-service customers, not everyone connected to its distribution system or every part of a customer’s bill.
  • Coverage check: total customers in each customer type are the sum of bundled and delivery-only customers reported in PUDL’s EIA-861 yearly sales table. The displayed share is bundled customers divided by that total. The 1,018 populated EIA price-table rows reconcile exactly with PUDL’s bundled counts. Missing published prices remain blank.
  • Coverage flag: “minority coverage” means the published bundled price applies to less than 50% of bundled plus delivery-only customers in the same customer class. The flag does not change the price. The majority-coverage view is a project sensitivity check, not an EIA quality standard.
  • Ownership summary: the annual line is the unweighted median of the 30 panel utilities, selected as ten utilities in each 2024 ownership group. The shaded area is their observed minimum-to-maximum range. Utilities are grouped by their ownership code in that year, so an earlier group can contain a different number. These results do not describe every utility in the region.
  • Authorized return on equity: the matched-state pilot uses the ROE approved by Connecticut PURA or Massachusetts DPU for the shareholder-funded portion of rate base. Base ROE, effective ROE after an in-force adjustment, and actual earned ROE remain separate in the downloadable data. The chart shows effective authorized ROE in force at year-end. The derived dollar value is an authorized common-equity return component calculated only when the decision reports rate base and the approved equity ratio. It is not shareholder compensation, dividends, a customer price, or total company profit.
  • Regional generation: annual EIA-923 net generation at plants assigned to the NYIS or ISNE balancing authority. Shares are based on eight nonnegative fuel groups. PUDL’s “other” group is zero or slightly negative in these years and is excluded from the percentage stack; the excluded amount is less than 0.02% of annual positive generation. Imports and utility-specific power contracts are not represented.
  • Sample: the ten largest qualifying utilities in each 2024 ownership group, ranked by residential customers in the regional PUDL roster. This gives 30 utilities and 360 possible utility-years from 2013 through 2024. It is a deliberately large-utility sample, not a random or fully representative sample of every utility in the region.
  • Panel coverage: residential and commercial prices are available for 347 of 360 utility-years; industrial prices are available for 324. The reliability audit has a populated source row for 321; missing measures are left blank.
  • Reliability: utility-reported SAIDI and SAIFI from EIA Form 861, standardized through PUDL’s yearly reliability table. PUDL derives CAIDI as SAIDI divided by SAIFI from those reported values. The default view excludes major events; the alternate view includes them.
  • Reliability limits: Peabody and Fishers Island have no populated reliability rows in this period; Taunton is blank in 2013–2015; the current FirstEnergy entity is blank before 2024; and Central Hudson is blank in 2014. Some reported rows also omit individual measures. Six utilities change reporting methods during the panel, so reliability ownership-row medians are not shown. EIA allows either IEEE or another utility-defined method; Non-IEEE values are kept for within-utility description but are not treated as directly comparable to IEEE values.
  • Source check: for the selected utility rows, 2,841 fields from the official 2013–2024 EIA reliability workbooks matched the PUDL values exactly. Regulator reports then identified two NSTAR source conflicts: routine SAIFI and derived CAIDI in 2017, and the full 2023 EIA row. Those published values remain in the downloadable audit but are withheld from the charts. The reasons and regulator links are stored with each affected row.
  • Ownership: project-coded from documented ultimate-parent evidence. United Illuminating is DOM through 2015 and MTC from 2016; Narragansett Electric is MTC through 2021 and DOM from 2022. Central Hudson’s Fortis acquisition closed in 2013, so the project’s end-of-year rule codes it MTC for every displayed year.
