Methodology & caveats

  • Data sources: PUDL's out_eia923__yearly_generation_fuel_combined table (EIA Form 923) for generation by fuel, joined to core_eia860__scd_plants (EIA Form 860) for each plant's balancing authority, queried via DuckDB.
  • Years: 2020–2024.
  • Utilities: filtered by exact EIA utility ID, not name matching, to avoid mixing in unrelated same-named subsidiaries (e.g. Con Edison's merchant generation arm).
  • These are 4 specific regulated operating utilities, not their parent holding companies. "NSTAR Electric" here is Eversource Energy's Massachusetts electric utility subsidiary only — it excludes Connecticut Light & Power, Public Service of New Hampshire, and the rest of Eversource. "Con Edison" here is Consolidated Edison Company of New York (CECONY), not the publicly-traded parent Consolidated Edison, Inc. NYSEG and United Illuminating are both Avangrid subsidiaries, so they are not independent competitors at the parent-company level — comparing all 4 charts as if they were 4 unrelated companies would be a mistake.
  • Limitation (utility-owned section): this shows generation reported directly under each utility's own EIA identity only. It does not allocate jointly-owned plants by ownership share or roll up parent/subsidiary companies, so it is not the full generation mix delivered to retail customers — several of these utilities own little direct generation today and buy most of their power on the wholesale market. PUDL's generation-fuel table uses canonicalized EIA utility identifiers reconciled across multiple EIA sources; see PUDL's data dictionary for how that canonicalization works.
  • Why the regional section exists: because of the above, the utility-owned charts alone don't explain these utilities' costs. The regional grid mix (NYISO / ISO-NE) reflects the wholesale market these utilities actually buy from, which is the more relevant dataset for a cost/revenue analysis.
