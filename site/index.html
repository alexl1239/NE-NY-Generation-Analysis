Does utility ownership relate to electricity prices?

This project compares published prices for 42 utilities and reviewed reliability data for 30 utilities. It tests whether differences remain after accounting for state and year. The results show associations, not proof that ownership caused an outcome.

What the analysis shows

Clearest price result

COOP utilities have lower adjusted prices

The estimated differences are 6.64–10.87 cents per kWh below DOM utilities across all three customer classes. Every 95% confidence interval stays below zero.

Mixed price result

MTC results depend on customer class

Commercial prices are an estimated 4.09 cents per kWh lower than DOM. Residential and industrial estimates also point lower, but their 95% confidence intervals cross zero.

Reliability result

No clear ownership difference in routine SAIDI

The adjusted MTC and COOP estimates are higher than DOM, but both 95% confidence intervals include zero. This sample does not establish a reliable ownership difference in outage minutes.

Bottom line: ownership is associated with price differences in this selected sample, especially for COOP utilities. The design does not identify why those differences exist and should not be read as a causal test.

Data, methods, and limits

Data

Prices come from EIA Tables 6–8. Reliability comes from EIA Form 861 and was checked against all selected official workbook fields. Ownership is coded from documented ultimate-parent evidence using the owner in place on December 31 of each year. The allowed categories are MTC, DOM, SOE, and COOP; no selected utility is SOE.

Models

Price models include ownership, state, and year. The reliability model also includes reporting method. Each utility-year counts once and uncertainty is clustered by utility.

Limits

The samples are selected, not random. The models do not control every difference in regulation, customer mix, infrastructure, or weather. Results are descriptive associations, not causal effects.
